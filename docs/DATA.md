## Trade-offs worth defending
- **`(symbol, filing_date)` as the key** keeps one evaluation per filing, so a recompute overwrites in place. That is why history cannot be audited unless the `--recompute` run (PR #91) writes its before/after CSV report first.
- **Verdict in two columns** (`is_compliant` boolean and `verdict` text): the boolean stays for old consumers and is `NULL` for unknown; the text carries the full state (`compliant`, `non-compliant`, `insufficient-data`, `needs-review`).
- **Watchlists in Postgres** (D-019) replaced the file store, so several API workers or hosts no longer lose updates.
- **The alert mailbox on the watch row** (D-031) instead of an outbox table: one row per (user, symbol) holds both the observed and the notified verdict, so a pending alert cannot outlive or duplicate its watch, and a verdict that flips back before the send leaves nothing to send. The cost: no history of sent alerts (the mail log is the only record), and at-least-once delivery.
- **One publication path under an advisory lock** instead of a `blocked` column or a trigger (D-027): `pg_advisory_xact_lock` on the filing serialises publication and issue writes with no migration; the cost is one issue read per filing response, also on a cache hit.
- **Access as a fold over grants** instead of a stored renewal date: a queued purchase or a refund never rewrites another purchase's dates, and there is no promotion job (D-024). Lifetime is an overlay from its settlement rather than a queued grant, so timed grants keep their wall-clock dates underneath and a Lifetime refund reveals whatever remains (D-024 amendment).
- **Pricing terms on the order** (D-030): the upgrade credit and its evidence are frozen on `razorpay_orders` when the order is created, so settlement re-checks a stored, auditable price instead of re-pricing, and the CHECK constraint keeps `amount = list_amount - credit_amount`.
- **Ratios as JSONB** keep each evaluation self-describing (value, threshold, passed, note, provider).
